## Research must be visible AND fun
Three **direct gameplay** studies:
1. **Dumb Ways to Die**: instantly graspable comic microgames, surprise and short challenges.
2. **Brotato**: inter-round drafting, build synergies, replayable pressure.
3. **Job Simulator**: office props worth playing with even without a point system.

**Papers, Please** is a **secondary philosophy, lore and world-building** influence only: bureaucratic rules, systemic storytelling, ethical nuance; never require its slow document-checking procedure.

**Central design question:** "How can quick microgames, build-making and tactile toy interactions turn an ordinary office job into an absorbing game?" The answer must be demonstrated in the game itself. The darkly funny employer manipulation remains the fiction, not an obligatory academic thesis.

Suggested actual design-author rationale, grounded in the expressed creative direction (not invented personal biography): **"I wanted office work to be enjoyable to touch, not just a list of tasks. Combining microgames with upgrades let one silly job change how the next one plays."** Fit a single *why-now* line during a natural first promotion: **"What if the little actions you master in one challenge changed every challenge after it?"** Credited optional direct links are accessible by the first promotion. These texts are editorial proposals, not claims of having conducted user interviews or personal playtests.

Contemporary culture argument (one concrete, defensible sentence): **"Games can transform work-like routine into play, but their familiar reward systems are only interesting when they give players meaningful choices and consequences."** Demonstrate both immediate fun and how rules affect agency. There is no requirement to make corporate ethics the sole research topic.

No compulsory citation slideshow, extensive tutorial, second academic mini-game, quiz, or courtroom ending. For each major game design inspiration include game developer/year and a direct clickable official or primary source in a compact in-game panel. Papers, Please may have a clearly separated narrative-philosophy credit.

## Pacing & independent reviewer test
Within first **15 seconds of CLOCK IN:** real target/action and feedback.
By **first promotion:** one completed physical interaction, interruption, a visible payoff and a chance to select a consequential upgrade; three game design studies cited in compact context.
By **middle of run:** a change of mechanical/cognitive register and a visible consequence without a speech. Alternate physically intense episodes, calm 1-gesture choices, and boss anticipation.
By **end:** an actual won fight and earned ending in the world. The two honest scores are a short joke and an optional close-up accounting panel, not a lengthy moral summation.

Review **real frames and recordings** against Night Shift HD, V3 and Workday. Inspect the standard four viewports including narrow 650x800. A scripted perfect playthrough does not prove human enjoyment; conduct a cold first-time observer playtest if genuinely available, otherwise label it untested.
